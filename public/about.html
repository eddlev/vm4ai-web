About VM4AI

Build AI systems around the work — not the theatre.

VM4AI is the home of AIR and the broader work behind it: designing practical ways for capable AI models to participate in sustained professional projects without pretending they are autonomous people, infallible systems, or magic.

Why AIR exists.

AIR began with a practical gap. Small companies and independent builders routinely need capabilities they do not have on staff: research, engineering, security thinking, documentation, positioning, analysis, review. Modern models can fill parts of those gaps, but ordinary chat is a poor project container once the work becomes long, consequential, or spread across sessions.

The problem was not how to make the model sound more like a professional. It was how to give AI work enough structure that the user can see what is active, what is assumed, what is allowed, what needs evidence, what still needs approval, and where the project should continue next.

AIR is the current answer: a prompt-based project runtime for governed, continuous AI work. It keeps the project contract explicit while leaving model capability with the host and decision authority with the human.

This page is about the people and principles behind that work. For the product mechanics, go to How AIR works. For the design lineage, read From Morphic to AIR.

Principles

The constraints are part of the product.

AIR is deliberately designed around what AI work can support honestly, rather than around the strongest story that can be told about it.

Transparency

Make the working state inspectable.

Scope, assumptions, gates, approvals, blockers, evidence posture, and continuation state should be visible enough for the user to challenge.

Evidence

Do not promote a record into proof.

Prompt-side state can document the contract. Claims about the external world still need evidence from the relevant source, test, tool, operator, or system.

Restraint

Stop where the evidence stops.

AIR should preserve material unknowns, block affected actions when required inputs are missing, and avoid converting plausible inference into settled fact.

Human authority

Capability is not authority.

AIR can bring specialist capability close to a task without quietly handing that capability ownership of the project or the user's irreversible decisions.

Continuity

The session is not the project.

Explicit project state should survive the container when a compatible host can accept and interpret the AIR foundation and continuation record.

Portability

Keep the core outside one vendor.

The project contract should not depend on a single provider's private memory, agent lifecycle, or hidden session state to remain understandable.
